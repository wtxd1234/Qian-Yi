# The Goal: Law of Conservation of Mass
In a chemical reaction, atoms are never created or destroyed. Whatever goes in (Reactants) must come out (Products). 

> **The Golden Rule:** You can **ONLY** change the big number in front (the **Coefficient**). You can **NEVER** change the small number at the bottom (the **Subscript**).

---

# Step-by-Step Example: $H_2 + O_2 \rightarrow H_2O$

## 1. Take an "Inventory"
Count how many atoms of each element you have on the Left (Reactants) and the Right (Products).

| Element | Left (Reactants) | Right (Products) | Balanced? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **H** (Hydrogen) | 2 | 2 | Yes ✅ |
| **O** (Oxygen) | 2 | 1 | **No ❌** |



## 2. Fix the Oxygen
To get 2 Oxygens on the right, we put a **2** in front of the $H_2O$.
*New Equation:* $H_2 + O_2 \rightarrow \mathbf{2}H_2O$

## 3. Re-Check your Inventory
Adding that "2" changed the Hydrogen too!
* **Left:** H = 2, O = 2
* **Right:** H = 4 ($2 \times 2$), O = 2

Now Oxygen is balanced, but Hydrogen is broken!

## 4. Balance the Hydrogen
To get 4 Hydrogens on the left, put a **2** in front of the $H_2$.
*Final Equation:* $\mathbf{2}H_2 + O_2 \rightarrow \mathbf{2}H_2O$



---

# Pro-Tips for the Exam
* **Save "O" and "H" for last:** Balance other metals and non-metals (like Carbon, Magnesium, or Iron) first. They are usually easier.
* **Keep Polyatomic Ions together:** If you see $(SO_4)$ on both sides, count it as one "thing" (one Sulfate unit) instead of counting S and O separately.
* **Double Check:** At the end, do a final count. 
    * Left side: $2 \times 2 = 4$ Hydrogens, 2 Oxygens.
    * Right side: $2 \times 2 = 4$ Hydrogens, $2 \times 1 = 2$ Oxygens. 
    * **Success!**
